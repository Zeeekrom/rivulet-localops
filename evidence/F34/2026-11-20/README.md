# F34 · Priority wording in the policy pack

## Result

The emergency (P1) and urgent (P2) wording moves from code into the policy pack's `diagnosis` section, so a council
changes what counts as urgent by approving a policy version. Policy v0.5.0 keeps the existing wording and adds two
urgent groups. The rules provider and the model path's priority floor (`rules_floor`, F28) both read it. On the 12-case
regression set exactly two priorities change, both P3 to P2 and both decided below.

| Situation | Level in v0.5.0 | Wording added |
|---|---|---|
| Blocked drain, water across a road | **P2** (P3 if water only pools; P1 if inside a building, already "flooding inside") | flooding the road, flooded road, road is flooded, road flooded, water across/over the road, flowing/gushing/flooding across the road |
| Dog at large, including near a playground | **P2** (evidence weaker, see below) | roaming dog, dog roaming, dogs roaming, loose dog, dog running loose, dog at large, stray dog, wandering dog |
| Construction noise at night | **P3**, unchanged | none |

## Sources

Researched on 2026-11-20 by a separate agent; every quote below comes from a page it opened, except where marked.

**Water across a road.** The council owns the public stormwater network. Hobart asks people to "Contact us as soon as
possible to report issues with stormwater systems" but publishes no time
(hobartcity.com.au, Stormwater and urban waterways, Issues and investigations). Councils that do publish times put a
hazard in a fast lane: Melton (Vic) responds "within two (2) working days if it is a hazard to the public", otherwise 10
(melton.vic.gov.au, Customer Service Commitments); Logan (Qld) gives "Initial response within 3 business days", or an
immediate response for a safety risk (logan.qld.gov.au, Key service standards). Water over a road is a traffic hazard,
so P2; pooling without a hazard stays P3. State roads go to State Growth's 24/7 line; floods to the SES (132 500);
risk to life to 000.

**A dog at large.** The *Dog Control Act 2000* (Tas) requires an owner to ensure "the dog is not at large" (s 16(1)) and
lets an authorised person "seize and detain any dog at large" (s 35(1)) (legislation.tas.gov.au). Hobart sends roaming
dogs to its online form and says to call Tasmania Police for "immediate risk to public safety"
(hobartcity.com.au, Animal problems and concerns). The only Tasmanian tiered table found, Glenorchy's, gives a 2-hour
response to animals "roaming or contained", but its page now returns 404 and the figure was seen only in a search
index: **unverified**. Following Hobart alone would give P3. P2 is chosen because a dog at large is a public-safety
matter under the Act; this is the weakest of the three decisions.

**Construction noise at night.** The *Environmental Management and Pollution Control (Noise) Regulations 2026*
(SR 2026-056, in force 14 August 2026, replacing the 2016 regulations) forbid listed equipment on weekdays "Before 7 a.m.
and after 6 p.m." (Schedule 1). The EPA sends construction-site noise to the local council
(epa.tas.gov.au, Make a report). Glenorchy aims "to investigate non-urgent complaints within approximately five business
days" and lists construction noise among them (gcc.tas.gov.au, Report an issue). It breaks a rule but endangers no one:
P3. Clarence and Devonport send out-of-hours noise to Tasmania Police.

No Tasmanian council publishes one response-time table covering all three. None of the standards found is close to the
8-hour target the demo attaches to P2; that label is a demonstration value and was not changed.

## Claim boundary

Synthetic demonstration policy, not City of Hobart policy. The levels follow public service standards; they are not a
council's own triage rules.
